\begin{abstract}
Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) fail in reproducible,
configuration-determined ways, and mechanistic-interpretability tools --- above
all sparse autoencoders (SAEs) --- have been proposed to diagnose those
failures. We built the complete pipeline the methodology prescribes: a 10-seed
failure atlas, TopK SAEs with mandatory PCA/random/probe controls, a
planted-feature positive control, a multiple-comparison-corrected causal
battery, leakage-audited early-warning monitors, and a rollback-capable
closed-loop controller. The result is a hardened negative: SAE-derived features
carry no causal information beyond matched controls, and the failure is
\emph{basis-independent} --- PCA components fail the identical battery, and no
candidate alignment satisfies the interchange criterion of causal abstraction.
The explanation is representational geometry: local tangent rank exactly equals
input dimension in all eight PDE families, while covariance participation
ratios stay far below layer width, so sparse dictionary learning solves a
problem that does not exist. Finally, we locate the regime boundary from the
other side: a Fourier neural operator trained on function-space regression has
participation ratio $6.8$ of $64$, and there the same TopK SAE \emph{beats}
$k$-matched PCA by $4.7\times$ --- the superposition signature absent from
every PINN layer. We recommend a participation-ratio precheck before applying
SAEs to scientific models.
\end{abstract}

\section{What To Do Instead}
\label{sec:alternative}
Participation-ratio precheck ($\rho = \mathrm{PR}/W$); linear diagnostics for
description; gradient/residual signals for prediction and control; SAE
methodology appropriate when $\rho \gtrsim 0.1$.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
Scoped negative; FNO result is reconstruction-level; power analysis (MDE
$85.7\%$ at 80\% power); controller not optimization SOTA (NTK-adaptive wins
on its target failure).

\section{Conclusion}
Check superposition before you sparse-code.
